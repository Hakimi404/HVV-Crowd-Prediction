Project Overview

How Hamburg Moves visualizes the pulse of Hamburg through open mobility data. Using HVV public transport records, traffic sensors, and event timelines, we explored how human behavior interacts with transport infrastructure. The project merges data science, visualization, and storytelling to portray a living city in motion.

The Visual Story

1. Predicting Crowds — Station Altona

This plot compares real passenger counts with forecasts from XGBoost and Random Forest at Altona station. Both algorithms successfully follow recurring daily peaks that correspond to rush hours, but deviate slightly on days affected by events or weather. Those divergences highlight the limits of statistical models when faced with human spontaneity.

2. When Events Change Everything

This chart contrasts hourly averages on weekdays with and without events. Event days show a prolonged evening surge between 16:00–21:00, matching concert and match times. Even distant stations show higher activity, proving that the ripple effect of major events extends through the entire transport network.

3. The City’s Heartbeat — Hamburg Heatmap

The heatmap visualizes how central hubs like Hauptbahnhof, Jungfernstieg, and Altona dominate city movement. Bright yellow circles indicate high passenger density; muted tones represent calmer suburban zones. It reveals Hamburg’s polycentric mobility: a dense, vibrant core with rhythmic outer loops.

4. Where Time Slows Down — Delay by Station

This set of bar charts breaks down average delays per mode. Hauptbahnhof remains the main bottleneck. The Metro performs best due to exclusive tracks, while Regional Rail and Bus are vulnerable to road or shared-track disruptions. The data underlines that physical infrastructure defines service reliability.

5. Unified Delay Overview — All Transport Modes

Combining all networks, we see Buses and Regional Rail have the highest delay averages, caused by congestion and inter-line dependencies. Metro lines show narrow, consistent ranges, confirming they operate independently from city traffic.

6. Hourly Delay Patterns — When the System Struggles

This line chart shows delay variation over 24 hours. Morning and evening peaks dominate, reflecting commuter flows. The Metro line stays almost flat, while Suburban Rail peaks earlier than Regional Rail — evidence of passenger transfer timing. Regional spikes around midday and late night suggest interference from intercity trains. It’s a clear daily rhythm of stress and recovery.

7. Why Trains Run Late — Delay by Cause

The dual heatmap highlights different causes. Suburban Rail experiences clusters around signal failures, medical incidents, and police interventions in the afternoon rush. Regional Rail shows systematic delays due to switch repairs and track closures during working hours — indicating planned maintenance rather than emergencies.

8. Forecasting Accuracy — Random Forest

This time-series compares real flow with Random Forest predictions. The overlap is strong for regular weekdays, showing high short-term accuracy. Deviations accumulate as prediction horizons extend beyond 30 minutes, where unmodeled events create divergence. The model captures rhythm, not randomness — a key lesson in urban forecasting.

9. Peaks and Events — Cultural Motion

This chart aligns event timelines with passenger surges. Nearly 80% of major peaks correspond to concerts, festivals, or football games. The model reproduces these surges well, proving that integrating event data enhances predictive accuracy. Culture is literally visible in Hamburg’s transport data.

10. Random Forest Predictions

Here, the model’s predicted and actual flows are compared across stations. The close match confirms Random Forest’s strength in pattern recognition. Slight underestimations during extreme surges reflect smoothing — the algorithm generalizes the trend rather than exaggerating anomalies. That stability is valuable for operational forecasting.

11. XGBoost Model Comparison

This chart compares XGBoost with Random Forest. XGBoost responds faster to abrupt changes but tends to overreact to short spikes, while Random Forest provides steadier output. The balance between agility and reliability makes them complementary choices for real-time passenger forecasting.

12. Cleaned Real vs Predicted Data

The chart shows how cleaned real data aligns more smoothly with predicted series. Noise filtering clarifies the genuine commuter pattern, removing short-lived anomalies. This preprocessing step improves interpretability and stabilizes model performance — a crucial step before training or validation.

13. Random Forest Error vs Horizon

The line plot tracks MAE and RMSE as forecast horizons increase. The error grows nonlinearly, showing that long-term uncertainty compounds quickly. This emphasizes a common truth in predictive analytics: near-future behavior is predictable, distant behavior isn’t. The model is reliable for 15–30 minutes, but confidence drops after one hour.

Dataset Information

This project uses multiple open data sources from Hamburg’s mobility ecosystem:

  • HVV Open Data Portal – Real-time and historical public transport data.
  • DWD (German Weather Service) – Weather data correlated with delay analysis.
  • City of Hamburg Events API – Official event listings used for crowd prediction alignment.
  • Traffic Flow Sensors – Aggregated road congestion data integrated with station metrics.

All datasets were cleaned, normalized, and synchronized at a 15-minute resolution for predictive modeling and visualization.

Projektübersicht

How Hamburg Moves verbindet Mobilitätsdaten mit maschinellem Lernen, um das Bewegungsmuster der Stadt sichtbar zu machen. Grundlage sind HVV-Daten, Verkehrssensoren und Veranstaltungsinformationen.

Die visuelle Geschichte

1. Prognosen am Bahnhof Altona

Vergleich realer Fahrgastzahlen mit Vorhersagen von XGBoost und Random Forest. Beide Modelle erkennen wiederkehrende Muster präzise, Abweichungen spiegeln unvorhersehbares Verhalten wider.

2. Wenn Ereignisse alles verändern

Veranstaltungen verändern die tägliche Mobilität deutlich – besonders zwischen 16 und 21 Uhr, wenn Konzerte und Spiele den Verkehrsfluss in der gesamten Stadt beeinflussen.

3. Das Herz der Stadt – Heatmap

Die Heatmap zeigt die aktivsten Knotenpunkte – Hauptbahnhof und Jungfernstieg leuchten, während Randgebiete ruhig bleiben. Sie spiegelt den Lebensrhythmus der Stadt wider.

4. Verspätungen nach Station

Der Hauptbahnhof bleibt Engpass Nummer eins. Metro stabil, Busse und Regionalzüge unterliegen externen Störungen.

5. Gesamtüberblick – Verkehrsmittel

Busse und Regionalzüge verzeichnen die höchsten Verspätungen, während die U-Bahn durch eigene Infrastruktur konstante Pünktlichkeit bietet.

6. Stündliche Muster

Spitzenzeiten morgens und abends, zusätzliche Ausschläge durch ICE-Verkehr – ein Abbild des täglichen Lebensrhythmus.

7. Ursachen der Verspätung

Nachmittags häufen sich polizeiliche und medizinische Einsätze bei der S-Bahn, während die Regionalbahn von planmäßigen Wartungen geprägt ist.

8. Prognosegenauigkeit – Random Forest

Fehler steigen mit dem Vorhersagehorizont. Kurzfristige Prognosen bleiben präzise, längere verlieren an Zuverlässigkeit.

9. Ereignisse und Spitzen

Etwa 80 % aller Spitzenwerte fallen auf Veranstaltungstage – Kultur ist ein zentraler Motor der Stadtmobilität.

10. Random Forest Vorhersagen

Tägliche Bewegungsmuster werden präzise nachgebildet; Abweichungen nur bei Feiertagen oder Events.

11. XGBoost Vergleich

XGBoost reagiert schneller auf kurzfristige Änderungen, ist aber empfindlicher gegenüber Ausreißern.

12. Bereinigte Daten

Durch Datenbereinigung verbessert sich die Übereinstimmung – klare Muster, geringeres Rauschen.

13. Fehler vs Horizont

Die Prognosegenauigkeit nimmt exponentiell mit der Zeit ab – ein Hinweis auf die Grenzen langfristiger Modelle.

Datenquellen

  • HVV Open Data – Öffentliche Fahrplandaten und Echtzeitinformationen.
  • DWD Wetterdaten – Korrelierte Wetterfaktoren zur Verspätungsanalyse.
  • Veranstaltungs-API Hamburg – Kultur- und Eventdaten zur Spitzenanalyse.
  • Verkehrssensorik – Verkehrsflussdaten zur Kontextbewertung.

Alle Quellen wurden auf einheitliche 15-Minuten-Zeitfenster normalisiert, um Vergleichbarkeit und Vorhersagequalität zu gewährleisten.
