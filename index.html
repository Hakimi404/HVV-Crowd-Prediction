Project Overview

How Hamburg Moves explores the real-time rhythm of Hamburg’s mobility. Using data from HVV public transport, city traffic lights, cycling apps, and event schedules, we analyzed how crowds evolve throughout the day — where they form, why they appear, and how they move.

Our four-week study combined real-world APIs and machine learning to forecast crowded areas, helping businesses plan smarter locations and residents avoid congestion. The result is a fusion of data science, urban analytics, and human movement — a pulse map of Hamburg.

Key Visualizations

1. Predicted vs Real Crowd Movement (Altona)
This line chart compares the predicted and real passenger activity in Altona between December 24–31, 2025. Two prediction models were trained: XGBoost and Random Forest. The orange line shows actual observations, while the blue and green lines show model predictions. Sharp spikes align with rush hours and pre-holiday surges, showing how well the models capture short-term crowd dynamics.

2. Average Result by Hour (Events Impact)
This chart contrasts weekday crowd levels with and without events. You can clearly see higher peaks in blue — during events, especially in afternoon hours (12–18h). It illustrates how cultural or sports events significantly amplify urban activity patterns across Hamburg.

3. Hamburg Heatmap
The map visualizes Hamburg’s top 15 stations (e.g. Hauptbahnhof, Jungfernstieg, Sternschanze) on December 8, 2025, at noon. The color and size of each circle represent the normalized crowd intensity per station. Larger yellow bubbles indicate heavy traffic zones — making the map a snapshot of Hamburg’s living heartbeat at that moment.

4. Highest Delay Stations
This multi-panel figure compares average delay times across transport types — Metro, Suburban, Regional, and Bus. Hauptbahnhof consistently leads in delay, followed by Jungfernstieg and Altona. It highlights how central hubs suffer the most time losses, revealing infrastructure bottlenecks at key junctions.

5. Delay Causes Heatmap
Each heatmap shows when and why delays occur, grouped by cause and time of day. For Suburban Rail, police investigations and medical emergencies dominate early mornings, while Regional Rail faces technical and track-related disruptions. These insights help identify the systemic patterns behind transport unreliability.

6. Prediction Accuracy Over Time
This panel shows how prediction accuracy changes with time intervals — 5, 15, and 60 minutes. The line chart at the bottom compares MAE (Mean Absolute Error) and RMSE (Root Mean Square Error). The models perform very accurately in short intervals (±1.2 people per 5 min), but error grows over longer periods, showing the trade-off between precision and time horizon.

7. Peaks Associated with Events
These bar charts show how crowd peaks correspond to public events. Stations like Bergedorf, Wandsbek Markt, and Kellinghusenstraße have over 60–80% of peaks linked to nearby happenings, proving the city’s mobility pulse is deeply synchronized with its event calendar.

Insights & Impact

The data reveals a clear story: Hamburg’s public life and movement are driven by time, location, and culture. Predictive modeling proves that human mobility follows consistent patterns, disrupted only by special events or unexpected delays.

From an urban perspective, this project demonstrates the power of integrating mobility data into smart city planning. By linking transport analytics with event and sensor data, Hamburg could forecast congestion and guide citizens — a step toward a more efficient and sustainable urban mobility system.
